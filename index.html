About Me

I am currently a Research Professor at Chosun Babylab and the Center for Data Science in the Humanities at Chosun University in Gwangju, South Korea.

My research focuses on early language development, particularly how children learn language through everyday linguistic input and social interactions. I use eye-tracking, naturalistic recordings, corpus analysis, and computational methods to investigate language acquisition in both experimental and real-world settings.

I am particularly interested in bringing together behavioural and computational approaches to better understand children's early language experiences, from the structure of caregiver speech to the development of word recognition and conversational abilities.

Research Interests

  • Statistical word segmentation
  • Caregiver–child conversational dynamics
  • Gaze-based word recognition
  • Automated analysis of daylong recordings
  • Early vocabulary development and assessment
  • Multilingual language acquisition

Research and Publications

My recent research examines Korean child-directed speech, caregiver–child interactions, word segmentation, and computational approaches to analysing naturalistic language environments. I am also interested in how automated speech analysis can support research involving large collections of everyday recordings.

During my PhD, I developed Bayesian IRT-based adaptive assessments for tracking early vocabulary development in multilingual children. These brief, browser-based tools were designed to support efficient assessment and targeted intervention, and have been integrated into e-Babylab.
