I am currently a Research Professor at Chosun Babylab and the Center for Data Science in the Humanities at Chosun University in Gwangju, South Korea.

My research focuses on early language development, particularly how young children learn from everyday linguistic input and social interactions. Using eye-tracking, naturalistic recordings, corpus analysis, and computational methods, I investigate language acquisition across both experimental and real-world settings. I am especially interested in how different approaches can complement one another in understanding early language learning.

My recent work examines Korean child-directed speech, caregiver–child conversational dynamics, statistical word segmentation, and automated analysis of daylong recordings. I also study early vocabulary development, including how children's word knowledge can be assessed through parental reports and behavioural measures.

During my PhD, I developed Bayesian IRT-based adaptive assessments for tracking early vocabulary development in multilingual children. These brief, browser-based tools were designed to support efficient assessment and targeted intervention, and have been integrated into e-Babylab.

Before joining Chosun University, I was a Lecturer at Sunway University, where I taught Biological Psychology and Research Methods. I enjoy teaching and working with students, particularly when it comes to developing research questions, exploring data, and learning how to make sense of findings.

You can find more about my work through my publications, conference presentations, and Google Scholar profile. Some of my research tools and projects are also available on GitHub.

I am also experimenting with a tool for testing emotion perception.

Outside of research, I enjoy spending time with my wife and our two daughters, reading, and making things out of wire. I am also learning Korean and exploring life in South Korea, with my family
